Your live score froze in the last over, 6 to win. Why does one expired cache key do that?
Design Simplified · 11: the thundering herd, told through one cricket score.

- Every phone reads the score from one cache key. When it expires, every request misses at once.
- What if 10,000 requests a second miss, and a refill takes 200 ms? That's 2,000 copies of one query on the database.
- Keeping the key longer doesn't fix it. It still expires, just later.
- Fix 1: the first miss gets a lease (a 64-bit token) and is the only one that refills the key.
- Fix 2: everyone else waits a few milliseconds and reads the fresh value.
- Fix 3: one lease per key every 10 seconds. Facebook measured peak database queries falling from 17,000 a second to 1,300.
- Twist: nobody has to wait. Serve the old score marked stale while the refill runs.

This cache stampede question comes up in system design interview rounds on caching and hot keys.
Standard interview design, not any score app's actual system. Lease numbers from Facebook's own paper, Scaling Memcache at Facebook (NSDI 2013).
