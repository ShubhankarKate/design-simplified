Your score froze, 6 to win | Cache stampede | System Design #11

DESCRIPTION
Your live score froze in the last over, 6 to win. Why does one expired cache key do that?

Design Simplified · 11: the thundering herd (cache stampede) and leases.

- Every phone reads the score from one cache key. When it expires, every request misses at once and goes to the database.
- What if 10,000 requests a second miss and a refill takes 200 ms? That's 2,000 copies of one query (our math).
- The naive answer is "keep the key longer". It still expires, just later.
- Step 1: the first miss gets a lease, a 64-bit token, and is the only one that refills the key.
- Step 2: everyone else waits a few milliseconds and reads the fresh value.
- Step 3: one lease per key every 10 seconds. Facebook measured peak database queries falling from 17,000 a second to 1,300.
- The twist: nobody has to wait. Serve the old score marked stale while the refill runs.

Lease numbers from Scaling Memcache at Facebook (NSDI 2013). Standard interview design, not any score app's actual system.
