DESCRIPTION
143,199 posts in one second: how does an app find the top 10 trending words without counting every one?

Design Simplified · 15: how a trending list finds the top 10 words in a flood of posts without counting every word, and the top-K heavy hitters question interviews ask.

- Counting every word exactly on one server breaks: Twitter's record is 143,199 posts in one second (2013).
- Keep a tiny grid instead: 5 rows of 2,719 counters (a Count-Min sketch).
- Each word hashes to one counter per row, and each gets +1.
- To read a word's count, take the smallest of its 5 counters. Collisions only push counts up, so it never undercounts.
- Keep a heap of the top 10; a word that beats the smallest gets swapped in.
- The whole grid is ≈ 54 KB.

Sources: Cormode & Muthukrishnan, 'An Improved Data Stream Summary: The Count-Min Sketch and its Applications'; Twitter Engineering, 'New Tweets per second record, and how!' (2013). Standard interview design, not Twitter's actual system.
